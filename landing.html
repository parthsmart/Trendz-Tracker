What students learn

Beads first. Then the numbers move in their heads.

Children start with a real abacus in their hands. With steady practice they learn to picture it, and calculate quickly and accurately without it. The same habits of focus and visualisation carry into Vedic Maths and the Rubik’s Cube.

Abacus mental maths

Addition, subtraction, multiplication and division on the abacus, then by visualising the beads. Builds speed, accuracy, concentration and confidence with numbers.

Vedic Maths

Short, elegant methods for multiplication, squares and more, worked on paper and in the head. A useful companion to school maths.

Rubik’s Cube

Pattern recognition, memory and patience, learned one algorithm at a time.

Inside a practice session

What a practice session can include

  1. 1
    Warm up on the beads. Short sums on the abacus, with the correct finger movements.
  2. 2
    Close the eyes, see the abacus. The same sums worked on an imagined abacus.
  3. 3
    Flash and speed rounds. Numbers flash on screen, and students race the clock for accuracy.
  4. 4
    Practise at home. Daily drills and assignments in Trendz Tracker keep momentum between classes.

Each rod holds one “heaven” bead worth five and four “earth” beads worth one each. The rightmost rod is the ones place.
